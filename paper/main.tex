\begin{abstract}
We develop a reproducible computational pipeline for continuation, topological classification and Floquet verification of periodic-orbit families in the planar Newtonian general three-body problem. The first validation target is the unequal-mass, non-hierarchical family reported by Li, Li and Liao. This manuscript is intentionally claim-gated: numerical discoveries are inserted only from frozen verification manifests produced by the accompanying software.
\end{abstract}

\section{Research objective}
The objective is to map connected families rather than accumulate isolated periodic initial conditions. We treat mass ratios, energy/scale and angular momentum as continuation coordinates; attach linear stability through monodromy/Floquet data; attach symbolic topology through syzygy and free-group/braid invariants; and represent bifurcations as verified relations between continuation branches.

\section{Published baseline}
Li, Li and Liao reported one family containing 135,445 non-hierarchical unequal-mass periodic orbits, including 13,315 linearly stable examples \cite{li2021stable}. Their public supplementary data provide the baseline against which the ATLAS pipeline is validated. The numbers in this paragraph are literature facts and are not presented as independent ATLAS reproductions.

\section{Equations and numerical formulation}
For planar positions $\mathbf r_i\in\mathbb R^2$ and masses $m_i>0$ with $G=1$,
\begin{equation}
\ddot{\mathbf r}_i = \sum_{j\ne i}m_j\frac{\mathbf r_j-\mathbf r_i}{\lVert\mathbf r_j-\mathbf r_i\rVert^3}.
\end{equation}
A periodic candidate satisfies $\mathbf x(T)-\mathbf x(0)=0$. Screening uses adaptive DOP853 in IEEE binary64. Scientific verification re-integrates candidates independently using arbitrary-precision arithmetic and precision/step refinement.

\section{Variational stability}
The fundamental matrix $\Phi$ obeys
\begin{equation}
\dot\Phi(t)=D f(\mathbf x(t))\Phi(t),\qquad \Phi(0)=I,
\end{equation}
and the monodromy matrix is $M=\Phi(T)$. Floquet multipliers are the eigenvalues of $M$. We report numerical pairing, trivial-mode and symplectic-consistency diagnostics together with the spectrum. Near critical crossings, classifications remain numerically ambiguous until resolved by precision escalation.

\section{Family continuation and topology}
Family identity is defined by connected numerical continuation, not by topology alone. Free-group words are stored raw, freely reduced, cyclically reduced and canonicalized under cyclic conjugacy; time reversal, inverse words and body permutations are retained as explicit transformations rather than silently quotiented.

\section{Discussion}
The publishable contribution of ATLAS is a traceable chain from candidate generation through independent numerical verification to family-level structure and bifurcation evidence. Machine learning is used to prioritize expensive verification, not to replace it.

\section{Reproducibility}
Every released figure and table is generated from a frozen manifest containing source-data hashes, code revision, arithmetic precision, solver configuration and per-orbit verification status. Screening artifacts are never used as discovery evidence.
