\section{Catalog, normalization, and notation}

The upstream table of Li, Li, and Liao contains $135{,}445$ unequal-mass
non-hierarchical planar periodic orbits on the mass grid
$m_1\in[0.8,1.1]$, $m_2\in[0.7,1.2]$ at $m_3=1$ with spacing $0.001$; the
source reports $13{,}315$ samples as linearly stable \cite{li2021stable}. The
archive used here accepts that table only at Git blob
\texttt{79b2963df43e62201c35690bfc22bec166132427}
\cite{eliaz2026archive}. Newtonian gravity is taken with $G=1$.

The scale-invariant coordinates are
\begin{equation}
T_{\rm si}=T\lvert E\rvert^{3/2}M^{-5/2},\qquad
L_{\rm si}=L\lvert E\rvert^{1/2}M^{-13/6},
\end{equation}
with $M=m_1+m_2+m_3$. The two maps used in the Letter are
\begin{equation}
\Psi(m_1,m_2)=(T_{\rm si},L_{\rm si}),\qquad
\Phi(m_1,m_2)=(a,b),
\end{equation}
where $(a,b)$ are the physical $4\times4$ Floquet trace invariants defined in
Sec.~\ref{sec:floquet}.

\section{Topological-word convention}

Every catalog member is published with the syzygy word $bABabaBAba$. Removing
the three binary-collision points from the shape sphere leaves a space whose
fundamental group is free on two generators; a collisionless periodic orbit
therefore determines a conjugacy class in $F_2$
\cite{montgomery1995geometricphase,moeckel2012regularization}. The published
word is already freely and cyclically reduced and rotates to $ABabaBAbab$. The
word is catalog metadata. Component identity in the Letter is determined by
continuation of periodic solutions.

\section{Continuation algorithm and branch matching}

Published samples occupy the collinear shooting chart
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\mathbf r_1&=(x_1,0),& \mathbf r_2&=(1,0),& \mathbf r_3&=(0,0),\\
\mathbf v_1&=(0,v_1),& \mathbf v_2&=(0,v_2),
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
with $\mathbf v_3$ fixed by vanishing total momentum. The shooting unknowns at
fixed masses are $(x_1,v_1,v_2,T)$. A neighboring catalog point supplies the
predictor; the corrector restores periodicity in eight-dimensional
center-of-mass-reduced coordinates. A connection is recorded when
bidirectional continuation converges and the terminal shooting charts match.

\section{Mass-adjacency graph and selected connections}

The $135{,}445$ sampled solutions form a connected mass-adjacency graph with
$270{,}087$ links of grid step $0.001$ and a spanning tree of $135{,}444$
edges. Twenty-six connections were selected as the strongest available tests
of branch separation:
\begin{enumerate}
\item five macroscopic bottlenecks of the spanning tree, including a cut
separating approximately $40\%$ of the tree;
\item the twenty largest chart discontinuities along the spanning tree,
continued with $6$, $12$, $24$, or $48$ substeps as required;
\item the long-range pair $(m_1,m_2)=(1.072,1.066)$ and $(1.100,1.121)$, whose
mass distance is $0.061717$ and whose invariant images nearly coincide.
\end{enumerate}
Bidirectional continuation succeeded on all $26$ connections. The most
expensive spanning-tree link required $24$ substeps; its endpoint-corrected
residuals were $1.20\times10^{-9}$ and $1.13\times10^{-9}$. A $647$-point
shooting-rank recensus, after re-correction of the $28$ lowest-rank samples,
left a minimum scaled rank ratio $1.08\times10^{-5}$ and maximum closure
$1.80\times10^{-8}$.

\section{Generic strict-periodic cross-check}

The long-range pair was repeated in a translation-reduced strict-periodic
formulation that does not impose the collinear-velocity ansatz. Both directions
converged, with closure and terminal-match residuals of order $10^{-9}$. The
remaining $25$ selected connections were continued in the published collinear
chart.

\section{Invariant-projection audit}

Centered finite differences of $\Psi$ on the published grid produce
$134{,}046$ interior Jacobian estimates. The normalized determinant takes both
signs: $113{,}514$ positive and $20{,}532$ negative. There are $590$ adjacent
determinant-sign-change edges, and their incident nodes form one connected
locus in the sampled mass plane. The smallest normalized singular-value ratio
is approximately $1.39\times10^{-6}$.

A nearest-neighbor search in standardized $(T_{\rm si},L_{\rm si})$ coordinates
finds $27{,}111$ rows whose nearest invariant neighbor is at least $0.05$ away
in mass. Eighty-six of those far-mass pairs have standardized invariant
distance below $10^{-4}$. The long-range pair above is one such near
coincidence. These counts identify a singular locus and strong degeneracy of
the sampled projection; they are a discrete audit of the published grid.

\section{Physical Floquet reduction and spectral invariants}
\label{sec:floquet}

The variational equation is integrated in canonical Jacobi coordinates and
reduced to the physical quotient $E^{\omega}/E$, with
$E=\mathrm{span}\{X_H,X_L\}$. The result is a $4\times4$ symplectic return map
$M_{\rm ph}$
\cite{yakubovich1975,howard1987linear,frauenfelder2023symplectic,galan2002figureeight}.
Define
\begin{equation}
a=\operatorname{tr}M_{\rm ph},\qquad
b=\frac{(\operatorname{tr}M_{\rm ph})^2-
\operatorname{tr}(M_{\rm ph}^2)}{2}.
\end{equation}
Then
\begin{equation}
\chi(\lambda)=\lambda^4-a\lambda^3+b\lambda^2-a\lambda+1,
\end{equation}
and, with $t=\lambda+\lambda^{-1}$,
\begin{equation}
P(t)=t^2-at+b-2.
\end{equation}
Spectral stability is equivalent to both roots of $P$ lying in $[-2,2]$. The
three physical boundaries are
\begin{equation}
G_+=b-2a+2,\qquad G_-=b+2a+2,\qquad
\Delta=a^2-4b+8.
\end{equation}
Their exact vertices are $(-4,6)$ (double $-1$), $(0,-2)$ (mixed $+1/-1$),
and $(4,6)$ (double $+1$).

The committed full-domain survey artifacts use the equivalent shifted
trace convention
\begin{equation}
\alpha=a+4,\qquad \beta=b+4a+6.
\end{equation}
In those coordinates
\begin{equation}
P(t)=t^2-(\alpha-4)t+\beta-4\alpha+8,
\end{equation}
and the same vertices are $(0,-4)$, $(4,4)$, and $(8,28)$. The labels
$G_+$, $G_-$, and $\Delta$ identify the same physical events in both
coordinate conventions.

\section{High-precision $+1$ transition}

On the slice $m_1=0.8023446113666945$, $m_3=1$, an independent $60$-digit
Verner integration with the physical quotient encloses the lower transition in
\begin{equation}
m_2\in[0.7540187211922449,\,0.7540187227698909],
\end{equation}
a bracket of width $1.577646\times10^{-9}$ with a sign change of $G_+$. The
smaller-$m_2$ flank carries a real reciprocal pair
$(\lambda,\lambda^{-1})\approx(0.9748,1.0258)$ and one elliptic pair. The
larger-$m_2$ flank places the formerly real pair on the unit circle near $+1$
at $0.99968\pm0.02547i$. Reduced closures are of order $10^{-21}$ and physical
symplectic defects are of order $10^{-19}$.

\section{High-precision Hamiltonian--Hopf transition}

On the slice $m_1=0.8022889780964406$, the upper transition is enclosed in

\end{equation}

\section{Full-domain critical survey}

The published table contains $620$ adjacent cells whose linear-stability label
changes. Each cell was localized once and assigned to a mechanism polyline:
$198$ of type $G_+$, $168$ of type $G_-$, and $254$ of type $\Delta$. Six
additional event-sign polylines from a full-domain sweep contribute $153$
samples, giving a thirteen-edge assembly in mass space. Of the $620$ published
transition cells, $462$ rest on binary64 localization and $158$ on
arbitrary-precision corroboration. The assembly is a survey of sampled event
preimages, not an exhaustive topology of every critical curve.

Two finite-lattice ends of the survey remain unclassified, at
$(0.892,0.75308)$ and $(1.042,0.85798)$. Orbits still close at those endpoints,
with re-corrected closures $6.6\times10^{-8}$ and $3.9\times10^{-8}$.
Continuing outward renders the event functional non-finite after travel of
$1.4\times10^{-3}$ and $2.2\times10^{-3}$ in $m_2$.

\section{Numerical sensitivity of the full-domain survey}

The $620$ published transition cells and $153$ additional sweep samples give
$773$ survey roots; adding the two headline representatives gives the $775$
localized records used in the residual summary. The largest stored absolute
event value is $1.9898\times10^{-8}$.

Re-deriving the monodromy through a coordinate rotation at identical tolerance
moves the event by more than $2\times10^{-8}$ on $345$ of the $620$ published
cells and by more than $2\times10^{-7}$ on $144$ cells. The changes concentrate
in $G_+$ ($126/198$) and $\Delta$ ($217/254$); $G_-$ changes on $2/168$ cells.
On a seeded sample of $160$ roots, closures stored from the screening
integration are smaller than closures remeasured at the event-evaluation
tolerance by a median factor $48.6$. Arbitrary-precision escalation of sixteen
difficult cells returned four as $\Delta$ collisions where binary64 had
recorded $G_+$. These observations motivate reporting the headline mechanisms
from the independent $60$-digit calculation.

A $C^1$ variational interval integrator was also tested at five seeds. Four
attempts returned no finite enclosure. The remaining enclosure was too broad
to distinguish the Floquet classifications and is retained only as a negative
validation experiment.

\section{Search and coverage diagnostics}

A raster over $m_1\in[0.997,0.999]$ and $m_2\in[0.993,1.012]$ at step
$10^{-4}$ comprises $4011$ samples, or $0.0253\%$ of the declared mass
rectangle. An additional twelve-point off-grid screen occupies $0.002539\%$ of
the rectangle. Even-order root pairs and tangencies are outside the reach of a
sign-change survey at these resolutions. These diagnostics bound the global
survey and are not used as claims in the Letter.
